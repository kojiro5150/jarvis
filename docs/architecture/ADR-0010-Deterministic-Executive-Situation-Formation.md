# ADR-0010 — Deterministic Executive Situation Formation

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 27 July 2026

## Context

The Executive Attention Layer deterministically identifies records deserving inspection, but it intentionally leaves separate policy matches as separate Attention Records. Downstream interpretation must not silently decide which records describe one situation. That grouping requires its own governed, replay-safe boundary before any interpretation, planning, specialist reasoning, or execution.

## Decision

Introduce Executive Situation Formation immediately after the immutable Executive Attention Queue. A registry validates and deeply freezes explicit formation policies and exposes them in locale-independent identifier code-unit order. The engine evaluates only immutable Attention Records, canonical identifiers and relationships, provenance, and snapshot identity. It does not consume model output, embeddings, similarity, runtime state, execution history, or planning artefacts.

The initial policy set contains exactly four policies:

1. **Same Canonical Entity** groups records with the same domain, stable entity identifier, and current snapshot.
2. **Explicit Parent Relationship** groups only shared parent, parent-commitment, or recurring-series identifiers represented by canonical records.
3. **Explicit Project / Role Reference** groups only shared canonical project or role identifiers.
4. **Singleton Formation** creates one situation for every otherwise unmatched record.

Non-singleton policies emit only groups of two or more records. A record claimed by more than one policy or group is ambiguous and aborts the entire evaluation. The engine never resolves ambiguity through registration order, precedence, ranking, or inference. Consequently every successful Situation Set preserves every originating Attention Record exactly once.

Situation identity is the URI-encoded compound:

```text
situation:<current snapshot>:<formation policy id>:<sorted attention record ids>
```

It contains no timestamp generated by formation, UUID, randomness, priority, or semantic digest. Memberships are ordered by Attention Record identifier. Situations are ordered by lowest Attention Record identifier, then policy identifier, then situation identifier. This order is canonical structure only and conveys no priority, urgency, severity, or importance.

Every membership records policy identifier, policy version, a deterministic reason with evidence, and the complete originating Attention Record. The Situation Set records current snapshot identity, source queue identity, ordered situations, and factual counts for records, situations, singleton/multi-record situations, and records by formation policy. Construction validates JSON compatibility, identities, ordering, uniqueness, membership consistency, summary consistency, and deep immutability before returning any value.

## Consequences

Future interpretation receives one canonical, inspectable unit without gaining permission to reconstruct hidden groups. Formation is deterministic across policy registration order and replay, unmatched attention cannot disappear, and overlapping membership cannot silently alter outcomes. Explicit ambiguity can reject otherwise plausible groupings; canonical contracts or policies must be clarified rather than applying an adaptive tie-breaker.

## Non-goals and future boundary

This decision introduces no meaning, semantic similarity, scoring, recommendation, briefing, planning, routing, persistence, notification, UI, API, specialist invocation, runtime orchestration, or execution. Future interpretation may consume Executive Situations, but it must treat canonical membership as immutable. Planning must never become a second hidden formation layer.

## Rejected alternatives

- Grouping in planning or briefing: hides a constitutionally significant transformation downstream.
- LLM, embedding, fuzzy, or string-similarity grouping: infers relationships and breaks deterministic replay.
- Policy precedence for overlapping groups: silently discards a valid competing claim.
- Dropping unmatched records: violates attention preservation.
- Random or time-based identities: prevents stable replay.
